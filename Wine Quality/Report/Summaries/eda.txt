Key Findings

- Wine quality scores range from 3 to 9.
- The dataset is heavily concentrated around quality scores 5 and 6.
- Quality score 6 is the most common class, representing 2,323 samples (43.7%) of the dataset.
- Quality score 5 is the second most common class, containing 1,752 samples (32.9%).
- Together, quality scores 5 and 6 account for more than 76% of all observations.
- High-quality wines are relatively rare:
  - Quality 8 contains only 148 samples.
  - Quality 9 contains only 5 samples.
- Low-quality wines are also uncommon:
  - Quality 3 contains only 30 samples.
  - Quality 4 contains only 206 samples.

Interpretation

The target variable exhibits a significant class imbalance, 
with the majority of observations concentrated in the middle-quality categories (5 and 6) 
and very few observations in the extreme classes (3, 8, and 9).

This imbalance is an important consideration for machine learning model development. 
A model trained on this dataset may become biased toward predicting the dominant classes 
and may struggle to accurately classify rare quality scores. 
Appropriate evaluation metrics and imbalance-handling techniques should therefore be considered during the modeling phase.
